What Awaits You

This is what it feels like

The Sipaliwini is not a destination you visit. It is a place that enters you — through every sense, all at once.

See

Endless golden savannah stretching to the horizon. The four peaks rising against an infinite sky. A flash of electric blue in the undergrowth. Stars so dense they look like spilled salt across the blackness. Petroglyphs glowing amber in cave torchlight.

Hear

The deep roar of howler monkeys across the valley at dawn. Water rushing over ancient rapids. The soft crackle of a campfire. Indigenous singing that has echoed through these forests for ten thousand years. The profound silence between.

Feel

The weight of your phone — gone. Cool river water on sun-warmed skin. Calloused hands from a day of trekking. The lightness that comes when you realise you haven't thought about work in three days. The quiet certainty of why you came.

The Journey

Seven days that reshape
who you are

The Cessna's engine sputters to life on the tarmac in Paramaribo. You watch the coast shrink behind you as you fly south — over jungle, over rivers, over terrain that has barely changed in ten thousand years. Each day is both an outer adventure and an inner development.

Day 1

Arrival at Four Brothers

Surrender — Letting go of the city

The Cessna banks left and suddenly they're there — four ancient peaks rising from the savannah like sleeping giants. You touch down at Sipaliwini village, met by the Trio people whose ancestors have called this land home since before recorded history.

You'll feel the butterflies of arrival, the strange relief of having no bars on your phone, and the quiet thrill of knowing you're at the edge of the map.

Day 2

The Sacred Ground

Perspective — Time measured in millennia

Trek through savannah at the foot of the Four Brothers, the mountains growing larger with every step. You arrive at two massive granite boulders rising from the plain, covered in petroglyphs carved by hands that held spears and painted bodies thousands of years ago.

You'll feel the humbling weight of ancient history and the profound smallness — and peace — of being under that sky.

Day 3

The Blue Ghost — Okopipi

Wonder — Reclaiming childlike curiosity

Today you go deeper, into the "forest islands" rising from the savannah, in search of something that exists nowhere else on Earth. The Trio call it Okopipi. Science calls it Dendrobates azureus. A creature the size of your thumbnail, glowing like a living sapphire.

You'll feel the childlike wonder of discovering a creature that exists only here — and you are among the few humans on Earth who have seen it wild.

Day 4

The Village & the River

Connection — Meeting others, meeting yourself

A wooden boat carries you across the Sipaliwini River through flooded savannah — water lilies, startled birds, the occasional caiman sliding beneath the surface. The Chief welcomes you. In the afternoon, an archery demonstration, then the cool, clear river as the sun drops below the treeline.

You'll feel the simple joy of human connection across cultures and the sensory perfection of cool water after a hot day.

Day 5

Cock of the Rock

Awe — Beauty that rearranges you

A half-hour trek through cathedral-like jungle. Your guide stops. Points. And there it is: the Rock Rooster — Rupicola rupicola — a creature so vivid, so orange, so improbably crested it looks like it was designed by someone who refused to be restrained by nature's palette.

You'll feel the electric jolt of spotting something wild and perfect, then the warmth of firelight and ancient rhythms.

Day 6

Rapids & the Night Hunt

Resilience — Building what you need

The river journey to Kwamalasamutu is an adventure in itself — rapids that splash over the bow, stops to fish and swim, and an afternoon learning to bind a hammock and pitch your shelter. After dark, a spotlight glides along the river. Eyes glow back from the banks.

You'll feel the rush of whitewater, the pride of building your own shelter, and the deep tiredness that comes from full days lived fully.

Day 7

The Caves of the Ancestors

Integration — Returning as someone new

Your final day begins with a visit to the Granman, Grand Chief of the Trio in Kwamalasamutu. Then the Werephai Caves: 350 petroglyphs carved into rock over millennia — the largest collection of pre-Columbian rock art in South America.

You'll feel the awe of touching something eternal. You came with a question. You're leaving with an answer. Or maybe a better question. Either way: you are not the same.

Practical Details

Everything you need to know

What is included

We handle the logistics so you can stay present. From the moment you land in Paramaribo until your return flight, every detail is curated for safety, comfort, and immersion.

  • All domestic flights and ground transfers
  • Experienced local guides and Jason Westlake as expedition leader
  • All meals, snacks, and drinking water
  • Camping equipment and hammock setup
  • Community fees and cultural activities
  • Pre-departure briefing and ongoing support

Who this is for

This expedition is for curious, open-hearted people who are physically mobile and emotionally ready to disconnect. You don't need to be an elite athlete, but you should be comfortable walking 2–5 hours per day in heat, sleeping in hammocks, and going without modern comforts.

A note on responsible travel

We travel lightly and with permission. A portion of every fare supports the Trio communities we visit, and we follow strict leave-no-trace principles throughout the journey.
